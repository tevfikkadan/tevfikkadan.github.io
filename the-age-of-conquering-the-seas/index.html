The argument

About Denizlerin Fethi Çağı

Denizlerin Fethi Çağı: Mare Liberum'dan Mare Dominium'a connects naval power, maritime law, technological change, energy security, global trade and monetary order.

Its central argument is that the legal principle of freedom of the seas has always depended partly on a physical reality: the difficulty of exercising sustained control over vast maritime spaces.

As the range, persistence and precision of maritime surveillance and strike systems expand, that physical reality is changing. The result may be a world in which high seas remain formally open under international law while states acquire growing capacity to monitor access, raise the cost of unwanted presence, influence seabed resources, dominate ports and chokepoints, and deny operational freedom across increasingly large areas.

The Strait of Hormuz, anti-access/area-denial systems, the changing balance of naval power and the transition toward a multipolar international order are among the cases through which the book develops this thesis.

Concept and research related to the book

Mare Dominium: Effective Maritime Control and the Transformation of the Freedom of the Seas

Kadan calls the emerging condition Mare Dominium: not necessarily formal ownership of the sea, but the capacity to establish effective maritime control through armed power, technology and strategic geography. The concept page explains the framework; the scholarly article develops it in academic form.

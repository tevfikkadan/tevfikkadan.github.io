The Argument

From maritime freedom to effective control

The book connects maritime law, sea power, global trade, technology, strategic chokepoints and the transition toward a multipolar order.

The contradiction inside Mare Liberum

The freedom of the seas has never distributed power equally. States with superior naval mobility have used open maritime space for military access, surveillance, commerce and power projection on a far greater scale than weaker coastal states.

The expansion of territorial seas, continental shelves and EEZs can therefore also be read as stages in a long struggle over how much strategic freedom the open sea should provide to hegemonic maritime powers.

Legal freedom, strategic sensitivity

An Exclusive Economic Zone is not territorial sea. Coastal states do not possess full sovereignty there, and navigation freedoms remain. Yet in practice EEZs are increasingly treated as strategically sensitive spaces for resources, security, military activity and strategic depth.

The book describes this tendency as the strategic territorialization of maritime space.

MARE LIBERUM → TERRITORIAL SEA → CONTINENTAL SHELF → EEZ → STRATEGIC TERRITORIALIZATION → EFFECTIVE CONTROL → MARE DOMINIUM
Core Concept

Mare
Dominium

Not necessarily ownership of the sea, but the capacity to establish effective maritime control.

Mare Dominium describes an emerging condition in which legal freedom may coexist with growing practical control. Persistent surveillance, satellites, unmanned systems, long-range precision strike and command of strategic chokepoints make it increasingly possible to monitor access, raise the cost of unwanted presence and constrain operational freedom.

The central question is therefore not whether the law of the sea disappears, but whether the physical conditions that once sustained maritime freedom are changing underneath it.

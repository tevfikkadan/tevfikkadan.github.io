The Age of Conquering the Seas

From Mare Liberum to Mare Dominium

TEVFIK KADAN · KAYNAK PUBLICATIONS

The Age of Conquering the Seas examines a historical question that has returned with new technological force: can maritime spaces remain legally free while becoming increasingly controllable in practice?

“History has not yet recorded the conquest of the seas.” The key word is yet.

The book traces the long struggle between Mare Liberum and Mare Clausum, then argues that persistent surveillance, precision strike, unmanned systems, sensors, coastal missile networks and control of strategic chokepoints are changing the material conditions on which the freedom of the seas has historically rested.

Kadan calls this emerging condition Mare Dominium: not necessarily formal ownership of the sea, but the capacity to establish effective maritime control through armed power, technology and strategic geography.

About the book

The book connects naval power, maritime law, technological change, energy security, global trade and monetary order. Its central argument is that the legal principle of freedom of the seas has always depended partly on a physical reality: the difficulty of exercising sustained control over vast maritime spaces.

As the range, persistence and precision of maritime surveillance and strike systems expand, that physical reality is changing. The result may be a world in which high seas remain formally open under international law while states acquire growing capacity to monitor access, raise the cost of unwanted presence, influence seabed resources, dominate ports and chokepoints, and deny operational freedom across increasingly large areas.

The Strait of Hormuz, anti-access/area-denial systems, the changing balance of naval power and the transition toward a multipolar international order are among the cases through which the book develops this thesis.
